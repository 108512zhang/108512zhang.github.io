About

A short introduction.

I am a research assistant and PhD candidate at the School of Chinese Medicine, Hong Kong Baptist University, working in the Infinitus–HKBU Joint Laboratory of Herb Polysaccharide Research. I joined the group in April 2022.

My research is in the structural chemistry of natural polysaccharides. I use chromatographic methods together with diagnostic enzymes to check whether a sample is structurally uniform before it is used in bioactivity or materials studies, and I combine monosaccharide composition, methylation analysis, oligosaccharide profiling and 1D/2D NMR to describe structure. Alongside this, I have experience in targeted and untargeted metabolomics and in the quality evaluation of Chinese herbal materials and patent medicines.

In a recent study we characterised a commercial polygalacturonic acid reference material and identified two co-purifying polysaccharides. This matters when such materials are used as standards: some of the properties that are measured may come from the mixture rather than from a single polymer.

Research

Three parts of the same question.

Before a polysaccharide is assigned a function, it helps to know what is actually in the sample. These are the three steps I work with.

01

Purity assessment

A single, symmetric HPGPC peak does not always mean a single structure. Diagnostic enzymes are used to check whether a peak shifts only with the enzyme that matches its backbone — a practical check for reference materials.

PurityendoP · BhRgI · endoG
02

Structural characterisation

Monosaccharide composition, methylation analysis, ABEE-labelled oligosaccharide profiling and 1D/2D NMR are combined to describe linkage types and sequence features of a polysaccharide.

StructureNMR · GC-MS
03

Function and materials

Macrophage assays (NO, IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α; MAPK and NF-κB) and rheology or SEM of Ca²⁺-crosslinked hydrogels are used to compare the fractions obtained from a sample.

FunctionRAW264.7 · Rheology
